You've done this before.
But never quite like this.

Buying your first home as a renter came with a quiet luxury you may not have noticed at the time — you only had one side of the equation to manage. Your finances, your timeline, your decision. No chain. Just you, a mortgage, and the keys.

Buying your next home is different. You're simultaneously a buyer and a seller — two transactions that must complete on the same day, each depending on the other. That's the chain. And it's the thing most people underestimate.

Why ChainReady exists. The financial complexity of a simultaneous sale and purchase is genuinely difficult to hold in your head. This page walks you through every step. ChainReady handles the numbers. Together, they give you clarity before you commit.
Step by step
01
Know Your Numbers

Before viewing a single property, understand your full financial position — equity, borrowing capacity, total costs. Not just the purchase price.

As a first-time buyer, the equation was simple: savings plus mortgage equals budget. Now your deposit comes from your sale proceeds, and both sides must align on the same day.

02
Prepare For Sale

To be taken seriously as a buyer, sellers want to see your property is on the market — or already sold subject to contract. Being sale-ready is a genuine competitive advantage.

Chain tip: instruct your solicitor before you accept an offer. They can begin gathering title deeds and forms while the sale is progressing, saving weeks later.

03
Search & Offer

Search with a clear budget and strong credentials. When you find the right property, your offer should be accompanied by evidence of your financial position and the status of your own sale.

Always ask: has the seller found their onward property? A seller without an onward purchase can slow or collapse the chain. This shapes how you negotiate and what timeline you can commit to.

04
Manage The ChainChain Critical

Once your offer is accepted, multiple solicitors, surveys and mortgage applications move simultaneously. One slow party can hold up everyone.

As a first-time buyer you had one solicitor, one mortgage, one survey. Now you have four solicitors — yours as buyer, yours as seller, your buyer's, and your seller's — all needing to move in sync.

05
Exchange ContractsChain Critical

Exchange is the moment the transaction becomes legally binding. You pay your deposit. A completion date is fixed. Everyone in the chain exchanges on the same day.

This is where chains collapse. Any party who can't exchange — due to survey issues, mortgage problems, or cold feet — can hold or break the entire chain. There are no penalties for withdrawing before exchange, which is why this stage carries risk. Once exchanged, pulling out means losing your deposit.

HOA: Exchange of contracts Buildings insurance from exchange
06
Completion DayChain Critical

Funds move from the bottom of the chain upward. Each link completes before the next. If you're mid-chain, your key release depends on funds arriving from your buyer before you can release funds to your seller.

Build this expectation in: delays earlier in the chain can push your key release from morning to late afternoon. Stamp Duty Land Tax is due within 14 days of completion — your solicitor handles this.

Key terms.

Chain
A sequence of linked transactions, each depending on the others to complete simultaneously.
Decision in Principle
A lender's conditional agreement to lend before a full application. Shows sellers you're serious.
Exchange
The legally binding moment. Completion date is fixed, deposit is paid. No backing out without penalty.
Completion
The day ownership transfers. Everyone in the chain completes on the same day.
Conveyancing
The legal process of transferring ownership. Handled by solicitors on both the purchase and sale sides.
SDLT
Stamp Duty Land Tax. Due within 14 days of completion. Calculated on your purchase price.
Equity
The portion of your property's value you own outright — sale price minus outstanding mortgage.
Gazumping
When a seller accepts a higher offer after already accepting yours. Legal in England until exchange.
